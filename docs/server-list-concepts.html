Reimagining the server list

Four directions for how you move between communities in Voxium. Each mock is the full app, not a sketch — the rest of the shell stays as shipped. Nothing here is implemented.

Must scale to 100+ servers Must not read as Discord's icon rail Voice-first identity — live rooms are Voxium's pulse ⌘K switcher already exists and stays in every option
Concept A · The Spaces Menu

No rail at all — the server is a workspace you switch, not a strip you scan

The left edge disappears. Your current space names itself at the top of the sidebar; clicking it opens a menu ordered by what matters: live voice first, then pinned, then unread. A hundred servers cost zero pixels until you ask for them. This is the Notion/Slack workspace model, made Voxium by putting live rooms at the top.

Concept B · The Activity Dock

A floating dock that shows signals, not a library

The rail survives, but its job changes: it never lists your servers. It shows at most eight things — where you are, what you pinned, and what is lit right now (unread, or a live voice room with an equalizer under it). Everything else lives behind the dashed button. A 6-server account and a 200-server account get the same calm dock.

Concept C · The Spaces Strip

Communities as tabs — the browser metaphor, not the guild wall

Servers move to a horizontal strip above the panels, like tabs in Arc: the active space is an expanded tab with a name, the rest are compact marks, the long tail collapses into a count. The whole left edge of the app is reclaimed — the sidebar starts at channels. Nobody associates a top strip with Discord.

Concept D · The Constellation

No list anywhere — a Home you fly out to, shaped by activity

The radical one. Day to day you switch with ⌘K and never see a list. When you do want the overview, Home opens a full-canvas map of your communities, clustered the way you think about them — cards sized by your activity, live rooms glowing with who's inside. The mock shows the Home view itself.

A way to decide

B is the pragmatic favorite — it keeps the muscle memory, kills the "wall of guilds" look, and is mostly an evolution of the rail you already shipped. A is the boldest simplification if you're willing to bet on search-first navigation.

B and D compose: the dock for everyday switching, the Constellation as the Home you land on at launch and the overview you open when you want to see everything breathing at once. C stands alone best on wide desktop screens.

All frames use the shipped voxium theme tokens (indigo surfaces, Electric Sapphire, periwinkle hairlines, squared avatars). Mocks are static HTML — no product code was touched.
